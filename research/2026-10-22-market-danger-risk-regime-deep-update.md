### 2. Two differently dangerous ways equities could fall

**Route A — inflation / bear steepening:** Brent/diesel freight and war insurance stay high -> breakevens and/or real rates rise -> Fed limited in easing -> equity discount rates up -> debt-heavy AI data centres / speculative uranium and critical-mineral developers reprice -> actual orders risk slipping. Gold can FALL first despite geopolitics as in today's data. Energy upstream/refining may outperform tactically, but overlapping oil holdings create concentration.

**Route B — credit/liquidation / Treasury rally:** AI cloud borrower CDS jumps and bond demand evaporates -> new GPUs/data centres/leases postponed -> bank/private-credit marks, HY OAS broadens and suppliers see delayed collections -> selling in semis and liquid mega-caps -> Treasuries rally (nominal yields fall) as equities fall. Gold could still decline initially under collateral liquidation before later responding to easier real rates. **Falling US 10Y does not prove Fed easing or a safe macro regime.**

**Interaction:** a return of sustained physical war premium during an AI credit unwind produces stagflationary credit stress, where stocks and gold miners both lose value despite strong past nominal commodity earnings. Public-sector debt and insurance are the weak link to watch. IMF's [October 2026 GFSR](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/GFSR/Issues/2026/10/13/global-financial-stability-report-october-2026) lists AI correction, sovereign yield shocks and leveraged NBFI liquidity as plausible transmission vectors — not predictions of an unavoidable crash.

### 4. Oil's critical contradiction: crude exported does not equal normalized fuel cost

Recent FT/Kpler reporting puts Gulf producer crude exports **across all routes excluding Iran** at **24.6m b/d over seven days to 18 October**, above a **23.3m b/d pre-war** reference, thanks to transport rerouting, ship-to-ship transfers and alternate corridors. But **Hormuz-only flows remained >one-third lower**, while oil-product export, refinery logistics, insurance and LNG remain stressed. This is NOT proof that every barrel is delivered cheaply or that refinery product scarcity has ended. [FT/Kpler report](https://www.ft.com/content/509c0ecf-625e-4473-96cf-e249edb7c86e).

US DOE's **21 October** exchange RFP for **up to 315k barrels ULSD** from its Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve is a planned regional temporary fuel **loan**, not a delivered 315k-barrel national SPR draw. [DOE original](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-authorizes-exchange-northeast-home-heating-oil-reserve).

**What to monitor:** Saudi Aramco actual official production/export receipts; bunker/insurance premiums and tanker tonne-mile; NY Harbor ULSD and diesel crack margins; product vs crude inventories; Qatar LNG cargoes; separately dated ICE Brent calendar legs and NYMEX WTI legs. The Live Desk oil F1/F2 **original-final contract receipts remain missing**, so near-term backwardation magnitude should not be asserted.

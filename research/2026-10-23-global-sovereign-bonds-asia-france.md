## France: fiscal weakness rather than ordinary rate contagion

France 10Y OAT **5.1415% peak on Oct22**, versus Germany ~3.4%; OAT–Bund reached **177.7bp**, nearer 2011 euro-area fragmentation levels. Investors are being paid more to own a French OAT than some higher-debt peers, including Italy; the euro currency itself is identical for OAT/Bund and does not explain the yield gap.

The [IMF July 22 2026 France Article IV](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/07/22/pr26255-france-imf-executive-board-concludes-2026-article-iv-consultation) forecast deficit **5.2% GDP in 2026** and debt **118.5% in 2026 / 120.3% in 2027**. [Allianz 9 October](https://www.allianz.com/en/economic_research/insights/publications/specials_fmo/261009-france-bond-market-rout.html) highlights outsized fiscal and domestic-political risk, below-normal OAT secondary depth and leveraged balance sheet unwinds. French-bank CDS and BTP/Belgian spreads **not yet verified** and should not be called contagion.

**ECB is not an automatic OAT insurer:** It has the Transmission Protection Instrument for *unwarranted, disorderly fragmentation* but evaluates macroeconomic/fiscal conditions and whether repricing is justified by fundamentals. The ECB's [10 September rate decision](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp260910~314e508016.en.html) raised deposit rates to **2.50%**, with the next meeting **29 October**. The market may be repricing genuine fiscal fundamentals, making intervention politically and legally harder. [Reuters explainer](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/explainer-why-ecb-unlikely-step-france).

**Price sensitivity example:** For a *hypothetical* seven-year modified-duration French OAT, another **+50bp spread widening** with unchanged Bund yield implies around **−3.5% price**, before coupon/convexity; a 50bp compression offers the opposite price benefit. Spreads could move further. A 5.1% quoted 10Y yield does NOT remove downside.

**Track:** OAT-Bund sustained spread 175/150bp **illustrative** stress/stabilization markers (not intervention thresholds), BNP/SocGen bank 5Y CDS, BTP-Bund/Belgium, auction bid-to-cover/tail, budget/2027 election, whether ECB describes repricing as unwarranted. **Trade posture: avoid direct long-duration OAT, explore Bund-only duration for EUR haven if EUR/MYR risk acceptable.**

## Malaysia investor FX hurdle — important

Compare **US 10Y 5.11% USD** against **MGS 10Y 3.88% MYR**, a **1.23 percentage-point nominal yield pickup** before taxes, hedges, dealing charges and price changes. If USD/MYR falls **2%** over a year (MYR strengthens), an unchanged bond-price, one-year *illustrative* USD coupon return of 5.11% becomes only **(1.0511 × 0.98 − 1)×100 ≈ 3.01% MYR**. That is **below the MGS 3.88%** baseline, before all costs. Currency changes can dominate a seemingly attractive 5% local bond yield.

Conversely, if USD strengthens sharply in a credit stress episode, a US Treasury ETF may outperform in MYR beyond its coupon and bond price change. **This is why US Treasuries are a sensible selective crisis hedge despite the FX risk**, but not guaranteed positive MYR real return.

Hedging the **foreign bond currency into USD** (e.g. Vanguard BNDX) still leaves **USD/MYR** risk, and forex forwards cost the local-vs-foreign short-rate differential/basis. The 5.1% headline carry cannot simply be captured plus full currency hedge at zero cost.

Hot US CPI: Oil Costs Rise, and the Fed Has Less Room

US inflation rose faster than forecast in September. Consumer prices increased 0.6% over the month and 3.7% over the year. Petrol explains much of the monthly rise, but the core reading gave the Federal Reserve a reason to worry.

The monthly headline met forecasts. The real surprise was core CPI: 0.3% against 0.2% expected, with the annual core rate at 2.6% against 2.5% expected. That matters for borrowing costs, stock valuations and the next Fed decision.

In early trading after the release, two-year Treasury yields jumped and Nasdaq futures fell more sharply than S&P futures. These were initial snapshots, not closing prices. I am raising the investment hurdle rather than declaring that the AI boom has ended.

Why This Is More Than a Petrol Spike

Energy prices increased 5.2% in September, with petrol up 9.1% and fuel oil up 15.3%. Petrol alone contributed about 0.337 percentage points of the monthly headline increase, according to the BLS. The IEA has separately warned that global diesel supply remains unusually tight.

There was also pressure outside energy. Shelter rose 0.4%, services excluding energy services increased 0.4%, and core goods rose 0.3% over the month. These categories were firmer than in August.

Food prices were unchanged overall, with supermarket prices down 0.1%. This is not an across-the-board price surge. The larger concern is what transport and energy costs do to businesses and households if they remain elevated.

What the Bond Market Is Saying

After the CPI release, Barron's reported the two-year Treasury yield around 4.91% and the ten-year near 5.29%. S&P 500 futures fell around 0.3%, compared with roughly 0.6% for Nasdaq-100 futures. Those numbers are early, time-specific observations rather than a complete closing-market picture.

A rate hike at the 28 October Fed meeting is now a more serious possibility, but it is not a certainty. We still need the other Treasury maturities, inflation-linked yields, the dollar and credit spreads to see whether the sell-off spreads beyond expensive growth stocks.

Oil, Power and AI: Different Risks

Oil producers, refiners and product-shipping companies may benefit from fuel scarcity. ExxonMobil, MISC, Valero, Marathon, Phillips 66 and Scorpio Tankers now deserve closer checks of actual margins and freight rates. Higher pump prices alone do not mean higher company profits or a good stock entry.

For Micron, Broadcom and high-growth AI suppliers, higher yields can lower what investors will pay for future earnings even when orders are healthy. For Eaton, GE Vernova and grid companies, physical demand remains attractive but their customers still have to finance projects. TSMC's earnings on 15 October should help distinguish funded demand from expectations.

What I Would Do With Power Stack

I have reviewed all eight holdings and all 46 watchlist stocks against the CPI shock. ExxonMobil and MISC retain their stronger underlying cases; DAYANG still needs operating repair. Tesla, Take-Two and the YTL-linked warrant do not gain a new buy case from this release.

I would keep the current scores and stock rankings unchanged. Before adding to any position, I want updated prices and valuation, actual company cash flow, a full post-CPI rates and credit picture, and tomorrow's TSMC and producer-price data. The new review is linked below for each ticker.

My view: inflation and funding costs now pose a greater hurdle, particularly for long-duration AI and consumer names. I would prioritise research into cash-producing energy, refining, shipping and grid suppliers, but not chase them without current prices and margins. No immediate buy, score or holding change is justified by CPI alone.

15 October, 14:00 MYT · Q3 earnings conference.

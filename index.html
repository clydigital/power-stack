AI Spending Survives, but Who Can Finance It?

On 22 October, QQQ lost 2.86% and semiconductor ETF SMH fell 5.99%, while equal-weight RSP rose 0.73%. The completed-session figures show a sharp change in what investors preferred, not proof that AI orders disappeared.

Traders Reality's reported argument is to separate the business from the trade. Meet Kevin's reported scenario adds a second risk: expensive energy can keep inflation and borrowing costs high even as investors seek safety in bonds. Both are creator interpretations recovered from earlier discussion, not a newly verified verbatim transcript.

Act 1 — When the market sells a good business

Traders Reality's lens starts with what investors expected. When money is tight, funds can sell profitable companies to raise cash. The useful question is whether customers cancelled orders or whether investors simply stopped paying the old price for those orders.

TSMC's 15 October quarterly report showed US$46.72bn revenue, 66.4% gross margin and 58.7% operating margin. Its fourth-quarter revenue guidance was US$52.3–54.3bn. Those reported operations still look strong; an attractive purchase price for the US-listed TSM shares is a separate decision.

TSMC Q3 issuer results · 15 Oct ↗ 22 Oct equity-session source and comparisons ↗

Act 2 — Credit distinguishes the weak from the strong

ICE credit data available through FRED for 23 October showed the lowest-rated CCC-and-below group at a 13.56% option-adjusted spread, compared with 3.38% for the broader high-yield index. The two figures measure different groups, not the borrowing rate for any single AI company. Weak-credit stress is clearer than a market-wide funding shutdown.

That distinction changes how I approach AI. A business already generating cash from paying customers is not the same as a highly leveraged data-centre project that needs another refinancing. Before calling the sell-off contagious, I want same-date investment-grade spreads, lender terms, issuer cash flow and a matched market reaction.

FRED · CCC-and-below spread, observed 23 Oct ↗ FRED · broad high-yield spread, observed 23 Oct ↗

Act 3 — The money behind the AI promises

Meta's 26 October filing described roughly US$35bn in launched offerings: US$22.5bn in common shares through forward sale arrangements and US$12.5bn in mandatory-convertible preferred depositary shares. A further possible US$60bn programme had not been established. Calling all US$95bn funded, or calculating final dilution from that headline, would jump ahead of the documents.

Anthropic's 26 October IPO filing reported a US$80.75bn first-half net loss, but roughly US$72.4bn was fair-value remeasurement of financing instruments. That is not the same as US$80bn leaving the bank account. The investor's harder question is the remaining operating cash requirement and the cost of compute contracts. SpaceX's reported US$40bn GPU debt financing also needs executed terms before it becomes a bankable comparison.

Meta SEC financing announcement · 26 Oct ↗ Anthropic SEC S-1 · 26 Oct ↗ Reuters · SpaceX financing assertion · 25 Oct ↗

Act 4 — Oil, the Fed and the bond trap

Meet Kevin's recovered argument looks to Iran/Hormuz diplomacy as possible relief for oil prices, inflation and bond yields. But a diplomatic headline does not tell us how many barrels or LNG cargoes actually arrived. Different Brent contracts and times have been quoted in recent research; I will not call that a verified price collapse or recovery without matching them.

The US Treasury's 26 October two-year auction sold US$69bn at 4.708% with 2.59 times bid coverage. Indirect allocations were lower, but direct bidders picked up more. That was somewhat soft demand, not proof that Treasury buyers disappeared. VGIT and especially TLT still need confirmation from five-to-thirty-year nominal and real yields, Fed policy and the oil response.

US Treasury official 2Y auction · 26 Oct ↗ 26 Oct MacroPulse oil-contract caveat ↗

Act 5 — What I would do with RM13,491

The seven-position target stays 50% US Treasury exposure, 35% equities and 15% MYR reserve, but these are planned weights rather than confirmed broker holdings. VGIT remains a staged duration candidate; TLT has more risk if long yields rise; SGOV is liquid USD exposure, not MYR cash. TSM is a strong-company, price-sensitive entry study; CF waits for its 4 November earnings; RSP needs broader earnings participation.

Before adding money, check actual Rakuten cash and positions. DAYANG remains an operating-thesis exit review, TSLA a valuation-and-weight review, and YTLPOWR plus 6742UW a combined exposure and warrant-terms check. No automatic sale or purchase follows from either creator's view. The next decision turns on credit spreading beyond weak borrowers, new issuer cash-flow evidence, matched oil shipments and post-Fed yields.
